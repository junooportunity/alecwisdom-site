On Monism

Alec Wisdom

On Monism explores how Plato's reorientation fractured an older, unified mode of knowing — what might be called the Western bicameral mind — and follows the consequences of that division through the history of Western thought. In the age of artificial intelligence, it marks the possibility of a return to monism as the quiet reemergence of a deeper coherence beneath analysis.

Releasing free — 2026

Three parts. The first traces the lineage — from Orphic descent and the healer-prophets of southern Italy, through the Presocratic revelation, into the Neoplatonic spring that has no source outside itself, and across its Eastern mirrors in Sufi and Advaitic thought.

The second part turns forward. What was lost in Plato's decisive turn toward abstraction, and what the recovery of cosmology, the imaginal, and direct transmission might mean now — in a world remade by artificial intelligence.

The third is a dense apparatus of notes in the tradition of scholarly commentary — sources alive, not catalogued.

360 pages. The whole thing is a circular conversation.
